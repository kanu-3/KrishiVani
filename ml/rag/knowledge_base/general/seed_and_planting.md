# Seed and Planting

## Topic
Seed and Planting

## Overview

Successful crop establishment begins with healthy planting material, appropriate planting conditions, and correct planting practices.

Poor establishment can result from:
- Low-quality seed
- Poor germination
- Incorrect planting depth
- Incorrect planting density
- Inadequate soil moisture
- Excessive soil moisture
- Poor soil preparation
- Unsuitable planting time
- Inappropriate temperature
- Damaged or diseased planting material

Good establishment improves:
- Uniform crop emergence
- Root development
- Early plant vigor
- Crop competitiveness against weeds
- Yield potential
- Consistency of crop development

## Seed Quality

High-quality seed should have:
- Good germination potential
- High physical purity
- Appropriate genetic identity
- Low levels of contamination
- Appropriate moisture content for storage
- Low levels of seed-borne pathogens where applicable

Certified or quality-assured seed should be preferred where available.

Seed saved from the previous crop should only be used when its quality and disease status are known.

## Seed-Borne Diseases

Some pathogens can be carried:
- On seed surfaces
- Within seed tissues
- In contaminated seed lots

Seed-borne pathogens can lead to:
- Poor germination
- Seedling death
- Early crop infection
- Uneven crop establishment
- Disease introduction into new fields

Using tested or certified disease-free seed can reduce this risk.

## Seed Storage

Proper storage helps preserve seed viability.

Important factors include:
- Low and stable moisture
- Suitable temperature
- Protection from insects
- Protection from rodents
- Protection from moisture
- Clean storage conditions

High temperature and high seed moisture can accelerate deterioration.

Seed should be stored according to crop-specific storage recommendations.

## Germination

Germination depends on:
- Seed quality
- Temperature
- Moisture
- Oxygen availability
- Seed depth
- Soil condition

Poor germination may result from:
- Drought
- Waterlogging
- Excessively cold or hot soil
- Deep planting
- Soil crusting
- Poor seed quality
- Seed-borne disease
- Chemical injury

## Planting Time

Planting time should be selected based on:
- Crop temperature requirements
- Local climate
- Expected rainfall
- Frost risk
- Growing-season duration
- Soil moisture
- Variety maturity
- Local production recommendations

Planting too early can expose sensitive crops to cold stress, while planting too late can reduce the available growing period or expose reproductive stages to unfavorable weather.

## Planting Depth

Planting depth should be appropriate for:
- Seed size
- Soil type
- Soil moisture
- Crop species
- Planting method

Very shallow planting can result in:
- Poor moisture access
- Weak anchorage
- Seed exposure

Very deep planting can result in:
- Delayed emergence
- Reduced emergence
- Weak seedlings
- Increased risk of seedling disease

Exact planting depth should follow crop-specific recommendations.

## Plant Spacing

Spacing determines:
- Plant population
- Light interception
- Air circulation
- Root competition
- Water use
- Nutrient competition
- Crop canopy development

Very dense planting can:
- Increase humidity within the canopy.
- Reduce airflow.
- Increase disease risk.
- Increase competition among plants.

Very wide spacing can:
- Reduce land-use efficiency.
- Increase weed competition.
- Reduce potential yield in some crops.

## Transplanting

Transplanted crops should be moved into the field when:
- Seedlings are adequately developed.
- Root systems are healthy.
- Weather conditions are suitable.
- Soil moisture is appropriate.

Healthy transplants should:
- Have normal green foliage.
- Have strong stems.
- Have healthy roots.
- Be free from obvious disease or pest symptoms.
- Show no severe nutrient or water stress.

Avoid transplanting visibly diseased or severely stressed seedlings.

## Hardening Seedlings

Seedlings grown under protected conditions can benefit from gradual acclimatization before field planting.

Hardening can involve gradually exposing seedlings to:
- Outdoor temperature changes
- Sunlight
- Wind
- Reduced irrigation frequency

Rapid transfer from protected conditions to harsh field conditions can cause transplant shock.

## Soil Preparation

Suitable seedbeds should generally provide:
- Good seed-to-soil contact
- Adequate moisture
- Appropriate drainage
- Sufficient aeration
- Suitable soil structure

Excessive soil compaction can restrict:
- Root growth
- Water infiltration
- Air movement
- Seedling emergence

## Planting and Irrigation

After planting:
- Maintain adequate soil moisture for germination and establishment.
- Avoid excessive irrigation that saturates the root zone.
- Monitor soil moisture rather than watering solely by schedule.
- Adjust irrigation according to soil, weather, and crop requirements.

Newly planted seedlings often have limited root systems and can be sensitive to short periods of water stress.

## Fertilizer at Planting

Starter fertilizer may be useful for some crops when soil tests or local recommendations indicate a need.

Avoid applying excessive fertilizer directly in contact with seed because high salt concentrations can injure germinating seeds.

Nutrient requirements should be based on:
- Soil testing
- Crop requirements
- Expected yield
- Local agricultural recommendations

## Planting Density

Target plant population depends on:
- Crop species
- Variety or hybrid
- Soil fertility
- Water availability
- Climate
- Production system

Plant population should be selected according to crop-specific recommendations rather than using one universal spacing rule.

## Disease and Pest Prevention During Planting

Before planting:
- Use clean seed.
- Inspect transplants.
- Clean trays and propagation equipment.
- Avoid moving infected plant material between fields.
- Use suitable crop rotation.
- Select resistant varieties where available.
- Remove diseased volunteer plants.
- Maintain field sanitation.

These practices can reduce the introduction and early establishment of pests and pathogens.

## Management

### Before Planting
- Select high-quality seed or healthy planting material.
- Confirm cultivar or hybrid suitability.
- Check seed quality and germination information.
- Test soil where appropriate.
- Prepare the seedbed.
- Confirm planting date.
- Check soil moisture and temperature.
- Plan irrigation.
- Plan crop spacing and plant population.
- Inspect transplant health.

### During Planting
- Follow crop-specific planting depth.
- Maintain appropriate spacing.
- Ensure good seed-to-soil contact.
- Avoid damaging seeds or roots.
- Avoid planting into excessively wet or dry soil.
- Provide appropriate starter nutrients where recommended.
- Irrigate carefully after planting if needed.

### After Planting
- Monitor emergence.
- Identify gaps or uneven emergence.
- Check for seedling diseases and pests.
- Maintain appropriate soil moisture.
- Control early weed competition.
- Replace or investigate unusually weak seedlings where practical.
- Record establishment quality.

## Common Planting Problems

### Poor Emergence
Possible causes include:
- Poor seed quality
- Incorrect planting depth
- Dry soil
- Waterlogged soil
- Soil crusting
- Inappropriate temperature
- Seed-borne disease
- Pest damage

### Uneven Emergence
Possible causes include:
- Variable soil moisture
- Uneven planting depth
- Compacted soil
- Inconsistent seed placement
- Poor seed quality
- Variable soil temperature

### Seedling Death
Possible causes include:
- Damping-off diseases
- Root pathogens
- Insect feeding
- Frost
- Heat stress
- Waterlogging
- Drought
- Chemical injury

## Immediate Actions

When crop establishment is poor:

1. Check seed quality and source.
2. Examine planting depth.
3. Assess soil moisture.
4. Check for waterlogging or compaction.
5. Inspect seedlings for disease or insect damage.
6. Check soil temperature where relevant.
7. Review planting date and weather conditions.
8. Review fertilizer or chemical applications made around planting.
9. Compare affected and unaffected areas of the field.
10. Consult a local agricultural expert if the cause remains unclear.

## Prevention

- Use quality-assured seed.
- Use healthy transplants.
- Store seed under appropriate conditions.
- Follow crop-specific planting dates.
- Use appropriate planting depth.
- Maintain correct plant population.
- Avoid planting into unsuitable soil conditions.
- Maintain good seed-to-soil contact.
- Prevent excessive soil compaction.
- Maintain suitable soil moisture.
- Avoid fertilizer or chemical injury.
- Inspect planting material for disease and pests.
- Maintain field sanitation.
- Use resistant cultivars where appropriate.
