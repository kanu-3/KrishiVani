## Symptoms

Leaf scorch primarily affects leaves but can also affect petioles, runners, fruit stalks, berry caps, and fruit.

### Leaves
- Small solid purple, reddish-purple, or brown spots may initially appear on the upper surface of leaves.
- Lesions generally do not have the pale or white center characteristic of common strawberry leaf spot.
- Tissue surrounding numerous lesions may become bright red to purplish.
- As disease progresses, infected leaf areas become brown and dry.
- Leaf margins may curl and dry, producing an overall scorched or burned appearance.
- Severe infection can cause large portions of leaves to die.
- Heavily infected plants may experience significant premature loss of functional foliage.

### Petioles, Runners, and Fruit Stalks
- Elongated sunken purple-brown or reddish-brown lesions may develop.
- Severe lesions can weaken or girdle affected tissues.
- Infected fruit or flower stalks may die when lesions completely girdle the tissue.

### Fruit and Berry Caps
- Fruit can occasionally develop discolored streaks or sunken lesions.
- Infection of fruit or flower stalks can interfere with fruit development.
- Severe infection can reduce the amount and quality of fruit produced.

## Disease Development and Spread

The fungus survives primarily on infected strawberry leaves and plant debris within or around the planting.

The pathogen produces spores when infected tissues become wet.

Spores are spread primarily through:
- Splashing water
- Rain
- Wind-driven rain
- Contact with wet infected foliage

After infection, new lesions develop on susceptible leaves.

Mature lesions can produce additional spores during wet periods, allowing repeated infection cycles throughout the growing season.

The fungus can remain dormant in dry infected leaves for extended periods and becomes active when sufficient moisture is available. ([extension.umn.edu](https://extension.umn.edu/gardening-minnesota/growing-strawberries-home-garden?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## Favorable Conditions

Disease development is favored by:
- Frequent rainfall
- High humidity
- Prolonged leaf wetness
- Wet foliage for approximately 12 hours or longer
- Dense strawberry plantings
- Poor airflow
- Presence of infected leaves and plant debris

Hot, dry weather can slow or temporarily halt disease development. ([extension.umn.edu](https://extension.umn.edu/gardening-minnesota/growing-strawberries-home-garden?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## Detection

Strawberry leaf scorch can be suspected when:
- Leaves develop small solid purple or brown spots.
- Lesions lack the light-colored center typical of common leaf spot.
- Leaf tissue between lesions becomes reddish or purplish.
- Leaf margins turn brown and dry.
- Leaves develop a characteristic scorched appearance.
- Petioles, runners, or fruit stalks develop elongated reddish-brown lesions.
- Disease becomes more severe following wet weather.

Leaf scorch can resemble other strawberry leaf-spot diseases. The solid purple spots, indistinct lesion borders, and surrounding red-purple leaf tissue help distinguish it from common leaf spot. ([ipm.ucanr.edu](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/pest-identification-and-confirmation-leaf-spot-diseases-of-strawberry/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## Disease Severity and Impact

Leaf scorch can cause:
- Extensive leaf spotting
- Premature leaf drying
- Reduced photosynthetic capacity
- Reduced plant vigor
- Reduced runner development
- Reduced flower and fruit production
- Reduced fruit quality
- Lower yield during severe infections

Severe disease during summer and fall can weaken plants and contribute to reduced strawberry production in the following season. ([extension.umn.edu](https://extension.umn.edu/gardening-minnesota/growing-strawberries-home-garden?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## Management

### Before Planting
- Use healthy planting material from reliable nurseries.
- Select cultivars with improved resistance or lower susceptibility where available.
- Establish strawberries in sites with good drainage.
- Provide adequate spacing for airflow.
- Avoid establishing new plantings from visibly diseased plants.
- Select planting locations that allow foliage to dry rapidly after rainfall.

### During Growth
- Scout leaves regularly for purple or brown lesions.
- Remove severely infected leaves where practical.
- Improve airflow through appropriate plant spacing and bed management.
- Avoid unnecessary overhead irrigation.
- Prefer irrigation methods that minimize foliage wetness.
- Remove weeds that create dense humid conditions.
- Avoid excessive plant density.
- Maintain balanced nutrition and appropriate soil moisture.
- Monitor disease development after prolonged wet periods.

### After Harvest
- Remove or manage heavily infected foliage and plant debris where practical.
- Maintain field sanitation.
- Remove old infected leaves where appropriate.
- Control volunteer or unwanted strawberry plants that may carry infection.
- Review cultivar performance and disease history before the next production cycle.

Removing infected leaves and reducing plant debris can reduce the amount of pathogen inoculum available for future infection.

## Chemical Management

Fungicides may be used when strawberry leaf scorch causes significant disease pressure.

Chemical management is generally most effective when integrated with cultural practices and initiated before severe disease has developed.

UC IPM notes that fungicide treatment may be used in conjunction with cultural controls where disease pressure warrants treatment. ([ipm.ucanr.edu](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/leaf-scorch-of-strawberries/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Fungicide selection and timing should consider:
- Disease severity.
- Weather conditions.
- Crop growth stage.
- Cultivar susceptibility.
- Current local agricultural recommendations.

Repeated use of fungicides with the same mode of action can contribute to fungicide resistance. Products should therefore be selected and rotated according to current resistance-management guidance.

Strawberry cultivars differ in susceptibility to leaf scorch.

Where available, cultivars with improved resistance or lower susceptibility can reduce disease severity.

Cultivar resistance should be verified for the specific variety and local disease environment.

## Immediate Actions

When strawberry leaf scorch is suspected:

1. Inspect leaves for solid purple, reddish-purple, or brown spots.
2. Check whether lesions lack the pale center associated with common leaf spot.
3. Examine surrounding leaf tissue for red or purple discoloration.
4. Check leaf margins for drying and curling.
5. Inspect petioles, runners, and fruit stalks for elongated brown or purple lesions.
6. Remove severely infected leaves where practical.
7. Reduce prolonged foliage wetness.
8. Improve airflow through appropriate plant management.
9. Monitor nearby strawberry plants for similar symptoms.
10. Consult a local agricultural extension or plant-health expert for treatment decisions.
