# Cherry Powdery Mildew

## Crop
Cherry

## Crop Aliases
- Cherry
- Cherry tree
- Sweet cherry
- Sour cherry
- *Prunus avium*
- *Prunus cerasus*

## Disease
Powdery Mildew

## Disease Aliases
- Cherry powdery mildew
- Powdery mildew of cherry
- Powdery mildew of sour cherry
- Powdery mildew of sweet cherry

## Scientific Name
*Podosphaera clandestina*

## Common Names
- Cherry powdery mildew
- Powdery mildew of cherry

## Hosts / Related Crops
The pathogen primarily affects:
- Sweet cherry
- Sour cherry
- Other susceptible *Prunus* species

## Symptoms

Powdery mildew primarily affects young leaves, shoots, and fruit.

### Leaves
- White to gray powdery fungal growth may appear on leaf surfaces.
- Young leaves are generally more susceptible than mature leaves.
- Infected leaves may become distorted, curled, or slightly twisted.
- Severely infected leaves may show reduced growth.
- Heavy infections can reduce the healthy leaf area available for photosynthesis.

### Shoots
- Young green shoots may develop white powdery fungal growth.
- Severely infected shoots may become stunted or distorted.
- New growth can remain especially susceptible during periods favorable for disease development.

### Fruit
- Young developing fruit can occasionally become infected.
- Infected fruit may develop a white powdery coating.
- Severe infection early in fruit development can interfere with normal fruit growth and quality.

## Disease Development and Spread

The fungus produces powdery spores on infected plant tissue.

Spores are primarily spread by:
- Wind
- Air movement
- Contact with infected plant material

Unlike many fungal diseases, powdery mildew does not require free water on the leaf surface for infection. High humidity and suitable temperatures can favor disease development, while rainfall can reduce the movement and survival of exposed powdery mildew spores.

The pathogen can survive between growing seasons in infected plant tissue or buds depending on the local disease cycle.

## Favorable Conditions

Disease development is favored by:
- Warm temperatures
- High humidity
- Dense foliage
- Poor air circulation
- Abundant young green growth
- Excessive nitrogen that promotes lush succulent growth

Powdery mildew generally does not require prolonged leaf wetness for infection. Humid canopy conditions are more important than free water on leaf surfaces. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

## Detection

Cherry powdery mildew can be suspected when:
- Young leaves develop white powdery growth.
- New shoots become covered with white fungal growth.
- Young leaves become curled, twisted, or distorted.
- Symptoms are concentrated on vigorous new growth.
- Similar symptoms appear on multiple cherry trees during warm, humid conditions.

## Disease Severity and Impact

Powdery mildew can cause:
- Reduced leaf photosynthetic area
- Distorted young foliage
- Reduced shoot growth
- Reduced plant vigor when infection is severe
- Reduced fruit quality or development when young fruit becomes infected

Disease severity is generally greater when susceptible young tissue is abundant and canopy humidity remains high.

## Management

### Before Planting
- Select healthy planting material from reliable sources.
- Choose cultivars with improved resistance or reduced susceptibility where available.
- Establish trees with adequate spacing for air circulation.
- Select sites with suitable sunlight and drainage.
- Avoid excessive nitrogen inputs.

### During Growth
- Monitor young leaves and shoots regularly.
- Prune appropriately to improve light penetration and air circulation.
- Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilization.
- Remove severely infected shoots where practical.
- Maintain balanced plant nutrition.
- Avoid overcrowding within the orchard canopy.
- Monitor trees closely during warm, humid weather.

### After Harvest
- Continue monitoring trees for late-season infections.
- Remove severely infected plant material where appropriate.
- Maintain orchard sanitation.
- Review pruning and canopy-management practices for the following season.

## Cultural Practices

Important cultural practices include:
- Maintaining adequate spacing between trees.
- Improving canopy airflow.
- Pruning to increase light penetration.
- Avoiding excessive nitrogen fertilization.
- Removing severely infected shoots where practical.
- Monitoring young growth regularly.
- Maintaining good overall tree vigor.

Good airflow and reduced canopy humidity can help limit powdery mildew development. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

## Biological / Organic Management

Cultural control is an important component of powdery mildew management.

Useful approaches include:
- Maintaining good canopy airflow.
- Avoiding excessive nitrogen.
- Removing severely infected growth where practical.
- Selecting less susceptible cultivars.
- Monitoring plants frequently so management can begin early.

Specific biological-control products are not included in this initial record because sufficient evidence was not established for a specific recommendation.

## Chemical Management

Fungicides may be used when powdery mildew causes significant disease pressure or affects high-value plantings.

Chemical management is generally most effective when treatments begin early, before infection becomes extensive. Fungicides primarily protect healthy tissue and generally do not remove established powdery mildew colonies.

Where repeated fungicide applications are required, products should be rotated according to different modes of action to reduce the risk of fungicide resistance.

## Resistant / Tolerant Varieties

Cherry cultivars can differ in their susceptibility to powdery mildew.

Where locally available, selecting less susceptible cultivars can reduce disease pressure and the need for chemical intervention.

Resistance or tolerance claims should be verified for the specific cherry cultivar and growing region.

## Immediate Actions

When cherry powdery mildew is suspected:

1. Inspect young leaves for white powdery growth.
2. Check green shoots for similar fungal growth.
3. Examine young fruit for signs of infection.
4. Improve air circulation through appropriate pruning.
5. Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilization.
6. Remove severely infected shoots where practical.
7. Monitor new growth closely.
8. Consult a local agricultural extension or plant-health expert for treatment decisions.

## Prevention

- Start with healthy planting material.
- Select less susceptible cultivars where available.
- Maintain good canopy airflow.
- Avoid overcrowding trees.
- Prune appropriately to improve light penetration.
- Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilization.
- Monitor young leaves and shoots regularly.
- Remove severely infected plant material where practical.
- Begin management early when disease risk is high.
- Follow current local agricultural and pesticide recommendations.

## Related Crops

The pathogen primarily affects:
- Sweet cherry
- Sour cherry
- Other susceptible *Prunus* species

Related stone fruits may also have powdery mildew diseases caused by other powdery mildew fungi, but those diseases should not automatically be treated as the same pathogen or disease.
