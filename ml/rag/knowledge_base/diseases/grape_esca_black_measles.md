## Symptoms

Esca can affect leaves, fruit, shoots, cordons, canes, and trunks.

### Leaves
- Leaves may develop characteristic interveinal yellowing on white-fruited cultivars.
- Leaves of red-fruited cultivars may develop reddish or dark-red interveinal discoloration.
- Discolored areas may develop necrotic margins.
- Necrosis can progress between veins and produce a characteristic "tiger stripe" pattern.
- Severely affected leaves may dry completely.
- Symptomatic leaves may fall prematurely.
- Symptoms may occur on individual shoots or groups of shoots arising from the same cane or spur.

### Fruit
- Small, round, dark spots may develop on berries.
- Spots may be surrounded by brown, reddish, or purple halos.
- Fruit symptoms are often particularly visible on white grape cultivars.
- Infected berries may crack, shrivel, or dry.
- Early fruit infection may interfere with normal berry development.
- Severely affected fruit can become unmarketable.

### Shoots and Tendrils
- Shoots associated with infected wood may become weak or stunted.
- Spurs and shoots may die back.
- Severe disease can result in sudden drying of entire shoots.

### Trunk, Cane, and Cordon
- Internal wood may show brown or dark discoloration.
- Cross-sections may reveal concentric zones or dark spots within infected wood.
- Infected permanent wood may develop internal decay.
- Spurs and canes arising from infected wood may eventually die.
- Severe infections can result in permanent decline of affected vines.

### Apoplexy
- A severe form of Esca can cause sudden wilting and drying of the entire vine or large portions of the vine.
- Rapid decline can occur with little preceding visible foliar disease.
- This severe form is commonly referred to as apoplexy.

## Disease Development and Spread

Esca is a complex of wood-infecting fungi rather than a single-pathogen disease.

The pathogens colonize:
- Trunks
- Cordons
- Canes
- Spurs
- Other woody tissues

Fungal reproductive structures can develop in infected wood and release spores during suitable weather.

Spores can spread through:
- Rain splash
- Wind-driven rain
- Air movement associated with wet weather

Fresh pruning wounds provide important infection sites.

The pathogens can enter pruning wounds and gradually colonize the vascular and woody tissues. Wood infections may remain hidden for years before obvious foliar or fruit symptoms appear.

Symptoms are therefore often observed in mature vineyards even though infection may have occurred much earlier.

## Favorable Conditions

Disease development is favored by:
- Wet weather
- Rainfall during the pruning period
- Abundant pruning wounds
- Poor pruning-wound protection
- Warm growing-season conditions following infection
- Existing infected woody tissue
- Vine stress

Pruning wounds can remain susceptible to infection for several weeks after pruning, with susceptibility declining as wounds heal. ([ipm.ucanr.edu](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/grape/esca-black-measles/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## Detection

Esca can be suspected when:
- Leaves develop a characteristic tiger-stripe pattern.
- White grape cultivars show yellow interveinal discoloration.
- Red grape cultivars show reddish interveinal discoloration.
- Berries develop dark spots surrounded by colored halos.
- Affected shoots show dieback.
- Cross-sections of infected trunks or canes reveal dark or discolored wood.
- Symptoms repeatedly occur on shoots originating from the same cane or spur.
- Sudden vine collapse or apoplexy occurs.

Esca symptoms can vary considerably from year to year, so diagnosis should consider both foliar and woody symptoms.

## Disease Severity and Impact

Esca can cause:
- Leaf necrosis
- Premature defoliation
- Poor shoot development
- Fruit spotting
- Fruit cracking and shriveling
- Reduced fruit quality
- Shoot and spur dieback
- Progressive trunk and cordon decline
- Sudden vine collapse
- Reduced vineyard productivity
- Vine death in severe cases

Fruit and foliar symptoms may vary considerably between seasons.

## Management

### Before Planting
- Use healthy planting material from reliable sources.
- Inspect propagation material for signs of trunk disease.
- Select well-adapted planting material and suitable vineyard sites.
- Establish vines with sufficient spacing and good airflow.
- Develop a pruning strategy that minimizes unnecessary large wounds.

### During Growth
- Monitor vines regularly for tiger-striping symptoms, fruit spotting, and shoot dieback.
- Mark vines showing suspected Esca symptoms for later inspection.
- Remove severely affected permanent wood where practical.
- Avoid unnecessary injuries to trunks and cordons.
- Maintain good vine vigor through appropriate irrigation and nutrition.
- Inspect pruning wounds and trunk tissues when symptoms are detected.

### After Harvest
- Remove severely diseased wood where appropriate.
- Remove infected wood from the vineyard rather than leaving it as an inoculum source.
- Review pruning practices before the next dormant season.
- Plan pruning to minimize large wounds.
- Avoid pruning immediately before periods of expected rain when practical.

Pruning-wound protection and sanitation are important components of trunk-disease management. ([ipm.ucanr.edu](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/grape/esca-black-measles/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## Cultural Practices

Important cultural practices include:
- Starting with healthy planting material.
- Reducing unnecessary pruning wounds.
- Avoiding pruning immediately before prolonged wet periods where practical.
- Protecting important pruning wounds where recommended locally.
- Removing infected permanent wood.
- Removing severely diseased vines where necessary.
- Maintaining vine vigor.
- Avoiding unnecessary trunk injury.
- Maintaining vineyard sanitation.
- Monitoring mature vines for early symptoms.

## Biological / Organic Management

There is no reliably established biological treatment that eradicates established Esca infections from permanently infected grapevine wood.

Important nonchemical management approaches include:
- Use of healthy planting material.
- Careful pruning practices.
- Minimizing and protecting pruning wounds.
- Removal of infected wood.
- Maintaining vine vigor.
- Vineyard sanitation.

Current research continues to evaluate methods for reducing infection through pruning-wound protection and management of trunk diseases. ([grapes.extension.org](https://grapes.extension.org/grapevine-measles/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## Chemical Management

There is no generally reliable chemical treatment that eliminates established Esca infections from infected woody tissue.

Management efforts therefore focus primarily on prevention of new infections and removal of infected wood.

Some local agricultural programs may recommend products for protecting pruning wounds against wood-infecting fungi. Such products must be selected according to current local labels, vineyard conditions, and regulatory requirements.

Fungicides applied after permanent wood has already become colonized should not be represented as a guaranteed cure for Esca.

## Resistant / Tolerant Varieties

Differences in Esca susceptibility can occur among grape cultivars and rootstocks, but complete resistance should not be assumed.

The most important risk-reduction strategy is the use of healthy propagation material and careful management of pruning wounds.

Specific cultivar resistance claims should be verified for the cultivar and local disease environment.

## Immediate Actions

When Esca is suspected:

1. Inspect leaves for tiger-stripe interveinal discoloration.
2. Check berries for dark spots surrounded by colored halos.
3. Inspect affected shoots for dieback.
4. Mark symptomatic vines for further inspection.
5. Examine trunks, cordons, canes, and spurs for signs of wood discoloration.
6. Remove severely infected wood where practical.
7. Avoid making unnecessary pruning wounds during wet weather.
8. Plan pruning-wound protection according to current local recommendations.
9. Maintain adequate vine nutrition and water management.
10. Consult a local grape pathology or agricultural extension expert for confirmation and management decisions.

## Prevention

- Use healthy propagation material.
- Inspect planting material for trunk disease symptoms.
- Minimize unnecessary pruning wounds.
- Avoid pruning immediately before prolonged wet weather where practical.
- Protect important pruning wounds according to current local recommendations.
- Remove infected wood from the vineyard.
- Maintain good vineyard sanitation.
- Maintain vine vigor.
- Avoid unnecessary trunk and cordon injury.
- Monitor vines regularly for foliar, fruit, and wood symptoms.
- Develop a long-term trunk-disease management program.
