# Corn Common Rust

## Crop
Corn (Maize)

## Crop Aliases
- Corn
- Maize
- *Zea mays*
- Field corn
- Sweet corn

## Disease
Common Rust

## Disease Aliases
- Corn common rust
- Maize common rust
- Common rust of corn
- Common rust of maize

## Scientific Name
*Puccinia sorghi*

## Common Names
- Common rust
- Corn rust
- Maize rust

## Hosts / Related Crops
The pathogen primarily affects:
- Corn
- Maize
- Other susceptible *Zea mays* material

The fungus has an alternate host relationship involving several *Oxalis* species.

## Symptoms

Common rust primarily affects leaves but can also occur on other green plant tissues.

### Leaves
- Small pale yellow flecks may initially appear on leaves.
- Reddish-brown to cinnamon-brown pustules develop on leaf surfaces.
- Pustules may occur on both upper and lower leaf surfaces.
- As pustules mature, they can release large amounts of rust-colored spores.
- Older pustules may become darker and turn black.
- Numerous pustules can cause surrounding tissue to become chlorotic or necrotic.
- Severe infection can reduce the effective green leaf area.

### Other Green Tissues
- Rust pustules may occasionally develop on leaf sheaths and other green tissues.
- Heavy infection can weaken affected tissue and reduce overall plant vigor.

## Disease Development and Spread

The pathogen produces spores in rust pustules on infected corn tissue.

Spores can spread rapidly through:
- Wind
- Air currents
- Storm systems

The pathogen can produce multiple spore generations during favorable weather.

Long-distance movement of airborne spores can introduce the disease into new fields.

The pathogen has a complex life cycle involving corn and alternate hosts such as *Oxalis*. However, direct spread of airborne spores onto susceptible corn is important for disease development in production fields.

## Favorable Conditions

Disease development is favored by:
- Cool to moderate temperatures
- High humidity
- Frequent rainfall
- Extended periods of leaf wetness
- Cloudy weather
- Dense crop canopies

Common rust is generally favored by cooler conditions than some other foliar diseases of corn.

## Detection

Common rust can be suspected when:
- Leaves develop reddish-brown or cinnamon-colored pustules.
- Pustules occur on both sides of the leaf.
- Rust-colored spores can be released from the pustules.
- Older pustules become dark or black.
- Infection becomes more severe following cool, humid weather.
- Numerous pustules develop across the leaf surface.

## Disease Severity and Impact

Common rust can cause:
- Reduced photosynthetic leaf area
- Premature leaf senescence
- Reduced plant vigor
- Reduced grain yield when infection is severe and occurs early
- Increased physiological stress under heavy disease pressure

In many field situations, common rust causes limited economic loss because plants may mature before severe disease develops. Severe early infections can nevertheless become important in susceptible hybrids.

## Management

### Before Planting
- Select corn hybrids with improved resistance to common rust where available.
- Choose hybrids adapted to the local climate and disease environment.
- Avoid planting highly susceptible hybrids where severe common rust is historically frequent.
- Obtain quality seed from reliable sources.

### During Growth
- Scout fields regularly for rust pustules.
- Monitor disease development during cool, humid weather.
- Pay particular attention to susceptible hybrids and young plants.
- Maintain balanced crop nutrition.
- Evaluate disease severity and crop growth stage before considering chemical treatment.

### After Harvest
- Maintain general field sanitation.
- Review hybrid performance and disease pressure for future planting decisions.
- Use resistant hybrids in subsequent seasons when appropriate.

Because airborne spores can travel long distances, post-harvest residue management generally has less importance for common rust than it does for diseases that survive primarily in local crop residue.

## Cultural Practices

Important cultural practices include:
- Planting resistant hybrids.
- Choosing hybrids suitable for local growing conditions.
- Monitoring fields regularly.
- Maintaining good crop vigor.
- Avoiding unnecessary crop stress.
- Monitoring disease during favorable weather.

## Biological / Organic Management

There is limited basis in this record for recommending a specific biological-control product for common rust.

The main nonchemical strategy is:
- Selection of resistant hybrids.
- Monitoring disease early.
- Maintaining overall crop health.

## Chemical Management

Fungicides may be useful when common rust develops early and reaches economically significant levels, particularly in susceptible hybrids.

Treatment decisions should consider:
- Hybrid susceptibility.
- Disease severity.
- Crop growth stage.
- Weather conditions.
- Expected yield.
- Local agricultural recommendations.

Fungicide applications are generally more useful when disease is detected early rather than after extensive leaf damage has already developed.

Repeated use of fungicides with the same mode of action can contribute to fungicide resistance. Products should therefore be selected and rotated according to current resistance-management guidance.

## Resistant / Tolerant Varieties

Corn hybrids differ significantly in susceptibility to common rust.

Genetic resistance is an important management tool and can substantially reduce the severity of disease.

Where available, resistant hybrids should be preferred in regions where common rust is a recurring problem.

Resistance should be verified for the specific hybrid and local growing conditions.

## Immediate Actions

When common rust is suspected:

1. Inspect leaves for reddish-brown or cinnamon-colored pustules.
2. Check both upper and lower leaf surfaces.
3. Determine whether pustules are releasing rust-colored spores.
4. Monitor the rate at which new pustules are appearing.
5. Note recent cool and humid weather conditions.
6. Identify the planted hybrid and its resistance level.
7. Assess whether disease is occurring early enough to threaten yield.
8. Consult a local agricultural extension or plant-health expert for treatment decisions.

## Prevention

- Plant common-rust-resistant or less susceptible hybrids where available.
- Select hybrids suited to local disease pressure.
- Monitor fields regularly.
- Scout more intensively during cool, humid weather.
- Maintain balanced crop nutrition.
- Avoid unnecessary crop stress.
- Use fungicides only when justified by disease risk and current local recommendations.
- Rotate fungicide modes of action when chemical control is required.
