## Scientific Name
No disease-causing organism is associated with the healthy condition.

- Healthy tomato
- Healthy tomato plant
- Normal tomato foliage

## Hosts / Related Crops
The healthy condition applies primarily to cultivated tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*) and suitable tomato varieties.

## Symptoms

A healthy tomato plant does not show characteristic symptoms of the diseases and pest damage represented in this knowledge base.

### Leaves
Healthy tomato leaves generally:
- Have normal green coloration appropriate to the cultivar and growth stage.
- Have an intact surface without characteristic disease lesions.
- Do not show widespread unexplained yellowing, necrosis, or tissue collapse.
- Maintain normal compound-leaf structure.
- Show normal expansion and development.
- Do not show characteristic mosaic patterns, target-like lesions, rust-like spots, or widespread stippling.

### Stems and Shoots
Healthy tomato stems and shoots generally:
- Show normal vegetative growth.
- Have healthy green tissue during active growth.
- Do not show unexplained cankers, dark lesions, or progressive dieback.
- Maintain normal internode development and branching.

### Flowers
Healthy tomato flowers generally:
- Develop normally according to the crop growth stage.
- Have normal flower structure and coloration.
- Do not show widespread browning or abnormal collapse.
- Develop normally into fruit when pollination and growing conditions are suitable.

### Fruit
Healthy tomato fruit generally:
- Develops according to the normal cultivar characteristics.
- Has normal shape, size, and color progression.
- Does not show characteristic disease lesions, rot, scarring, or abnormal discoloration.
- Develops normally through ripening.

## Disease Development and Spread

There is no disease development or pathogen spread associated with the healthy condition.

A healthy tomato plant can nevertheless become affected by:
- Fungal diseases.
- Oomycete diseases.
- Bacterial diseases.
- Viral diseases.
- Insect and mite pests.
- Nutritional disorders.
- Environmental stress.

Disease and pest risk can be influenced by:
- Infected planting material.
- Infected crop residue.
- Nearby infected plants.
- Prolonged leaf wetness.
- High humidity.
- Poor airflow.
- Plant wounds.
- Water stress.
- Nutrient imbalance.
- Presence of insect vectors.

Regular scouting is important because tomato diseases and pests can develop rapidly under favorable conditions.

## Favorable Conditions

Healthy tomato growth is supported by:
- Adequate sunlight.
- Suitable soil moisture.
- Good soil drainage.
- Balanced plant nutrition.
- Appropriate plant spacing.
- Good air circulation.
- Healthy planting material.
- Suitable temperatures.
- Effective weed management.
- Low disease and pest pressure.

Maintaining appropriate irrigation and avoiding prolonged foliage wetness can help reduce the risk of several important tomato diseases.

## Detection

A tomato plant can generally be considered healthy when:
- Leaves have normal green coloration.
- Leaves do not show characteristic disease spots or lesions.
- Plants do not show unexplained severe yellowing or necrosis.
- Stems and shoots develop normally.
- Flowers develop normally.
- Fruit, when present, develops normally.
- There are no obvious signs of significant fungal, bacterial, viral, or pest damage.

A healthy image-based classification does not prove that the plant is free from every pathogen, pest, nutritional deficiency, or environmental stress.

## Disease Severity and Impact

The healthy condition has no disease-related yield loss.

Maintaining healthy tomato plants supports:
- Normal vegetative growth.
- Photosynthesis.
- Flower production.
- Fruit set.
- Fruit enlargement.
- Normal fruit ripening.
- Fruit quality.
- Normal yield potential.

Plant health can change throughout the growing season as weather, pathogen pressure, insect populations, and crop-management conditions change.

## Management

### Before Planting
- Use healthy, disease-free seed and transplants from reliable suppliers.
- Select cultivars adapted to local growing conditions.
- Select varieties with resistance or tolerance to important local diseases where available.
- Establish plants in sites with good drainage.
- Provide adequate sunlight.
- Maintain suitable spacing for airflow.
- Avoid introducing visibly diseased planting material.

### During Growth
- Scout plants regularly for leaf, stem, flower, fruit, insect, and mite symptoms.
- Maintain appropriate soil moisture.
- Avoid prolonged drought stress.
- Avoid excessive irrigation and waterlogging.
- Maintain balanced plant nutrition.
- Control weeds that compete for water, nutrients, and light.
- Maintain good airflow around foliage.
- Use appropriate staking, trellising, or pruning where suitable.
- Avoid unnecessary mechanical injury.
- Monitor for insect vectors such as whiteflies when virus risk is relevant.
- Remove diseased material where appropriate.

### After Harvest
- Remove tomato crop residues according to local production practices.
- Remove diseased or heavily infested plant material.
- Control volunteer tomato plants.
- Clean and sanitize reusable tools and production equipment where appropriate.
- Review disease history before planting another tomato crop.
- Practice crop rotation where beneficial.

## Cultural Practices

Important cultural practices include:
- Starting with healthy seed and transplants.
- Selecting adapted and resistant varieties where available.
- Maintaining good plant spacing.

- Removing diseased plant material.
- Sanitizing tools and equipment.
- Practicing crop rotation where appropriate.
- Monitoring crops regularly.

## Biological / Organic Management

No biological treatment is required for a healthy tomato plant.

Preventive practices such as:
- Healthy planting material.
- Resistant or tolerant cultivars.
- Good crop sanitation.
- Maintaining plant vigor.
- Improving airflow.
- Appropriate irrigation.
- Conserving beneficial insects and mites.
- Regular scouting.

can help maintain plant health and reduce disease or pest pressure.

Specific biological-control products are not included in this record because a healthy plant does not require biological disease treatment.

## Chemical Management

No chemical disease treatment is required for a healthy tomato crop in the absence of a diagnosed disease or pest problem.

Unnecessary pesticide applications should be avoided.

When a disease or pest problem is identified, treatment decisions should be based on:
- Confirmed diagnosis.
- Disease or pest risk.
- Crop growth stage.
- Cultivar susceptibility.
- Current local agricultural recommendations.
- Current pesticide labels and regulations.

**Important:** Chemical recommendations, permitted products, formulations, application rates, resistance-management requirements, pre-harvest intervals, and regulations vary by country and can change over time. KrishiVani should use current, locally applicable agricultural guidance before providing specific pesticide instructions.

## Resistant / Tolerant Varieties

Disease resistance is not relevant to the healthy classification itself.

When selecting tomato varieties, however, resistance or tolerance to locally important diseases and pests can help maintain crop health and reduce future disease-management requirements.

Relevant resistance traits may include resistance or tolerance to:
- Early blight
- Late blight
- Bacterial diseases
- Tomato mosaic virus
- Tomato yellow leaf curl virus
- Other locally important diseases

Specific resistance claims should always be verified for the individual cultivar and local pathogen or pest population.

## Immediate Actions

When a tomato plant appears healthy:

1. Continue routine crop monitoring.
2. Maintain appropriate soil moisture.
3. Maintain good soil drainage.
4. Maintain balanced plant nutrition.
5. Maintain good airflow around the canopy.
6. Control competing weeds.
7. Monitor leaves for new spots, discoloration, curling, mosaic patterns, or stippling.
8. Monitor stems and shoots for lesions or dieback.
9. Monitor flowers and fruit for abnormal development.
10. Check periodically for insect vectors and spider mites.
11. Investigate persistent or newly developing abnormalities promptly.

## Prevention

- Use healthy seed and disease-free transplants.
- Select adapted and disease-resistant cultivars where available.
- Maintain adequate plant spacing.
- Improve airflow through appropriate plant management.
- Avoid prolonged foliage wetness.
- Prefer drip or base irrigation where practical.
- Maintain balanced nutrition.
- Control weeds and volunteer tomato plants.
- Monitor crops regularly.
- Remove diseased plant material promptly where appropriate.
- Sanitize tools and equipment.
- Practice crop rotation where beneficial.
- Monitor insect vectors when viral disease risk is present.
- Follow current local agricultural recommendations when disease or pest problems are detected.

## Related Crops

The healthy condition applies primarily to:
- Tomato
- *Solanum lycopersicum*

Related solanaceous crops such as pepper, potato, and eggplant are distinct crops and should not automatically be treated as equivalent to healthy tomato.
