## Hosts / Related Crops
The healthy condition applies primarily to cultivated strawberry plants, including garden strawberry and other cultivated *Fragaria* species.

## Symptoms

A healthy strawberry plant does not show characteristic symptoms of the diseases represented in this knowledge base.

### Leaves
Healthy strawberry leaves generally:
- Have normal green coloration appropriate to the cultivar and growth stage.
- Have an intact leaf surface without characteristic disease lesions.
- Do not show widespread unexplained chlorosis or necrosis.
- Maintain normal trifoliate leaf structure.
- Show normal expansion and development.
- Do not show significant disease-related curling or distortion.

### Petioles, Runners, and Crown
Healthy plants generally:
- Produce normal petioles and runners.
- Show normal crown development.
- Do not show unexplained lesions, rotting, or progressive collapse.
- Maintain healthy green or normally matured tissues.

### Flowers
Healthy strawberry flowers generally:
- Develop normally for the cultivar and season.
- Have normal flower structure and coloration.
- Do not show widespread browning or abnormal collapse.

### Fruit
When fruit is present, healthy strawberries generally:
- Develop normally for the cultivar and growth stage.
- Have normal size, shape, and color development.
- Do not show widespread rot, mold, lesions, or abnormal discoloration.
- Maintain normal surface and receptacle development.

## Disease Development and Spread

There is no disease development or pathogen spread associated with the healthy condition.

A healthy strawberry crop can nevertheless become affected by:
- Fungal diseases.
- Bacterial diseases.
- Viral diseases.
- Insect and mite pests.
- Nutritional disorders.
- Environmental stress.

Disease risk can be influenced by:
- Infected planting material.
- Prolonged leaf and fruit wetness.
- Poor airflow.
- Dense plantings.
- Poor drainage.
- Drought stress.
- Plant wounds.
- Infected crop residues.
- Presence of weeds or volunteer strawberry plants.

Regular scouting is important because strawberry diseases can develop rapidly under favorable weather conditions.

## Favorable Conditions

Healthy strawberry growth is supported by:
- Adequate sunlight.
- Good air circulation.
- Well-drained soil.
- Appropriate soil moisture.
- Balanced plant nutrition.
- Healthy planting material.
- Adequate planting spacing.
- Good weed management.
- Low disease and pest pressure.

Strawberries benefit from consistent moisture but perform poorly under prolonged waterlogging. Good air circulation also helps foliage and fruit dry more quickly after rainfall or irrigation.

## Detection

A strawberry plant can generally be considered healthy when:
- Leaves have normal green coloration.
- Leaves do not show characteristic spots, lesions, or extensive necrosis.
- Petioles and runners develop normally.
- Crowns show normal growth without unexplained rotting.
- Flowers develop normally.
- Fruit, when present, develops normally without obvious disease symptoms.
- There are no obvious signs of significant pest injury or plant decline.

A healthy image-based classification does not prove that the entire plant is free from every pathogen, pest, nutritional deficiency, or environmental stress.

## Disease Severity and Impact

The healthy condition has no disease-related yield loss.

Maintaining healthy strawberry plants supports:
- Normal leaf development.
- Photosynthesis.
- Runner production.
- Flowering.
- Fruit set.
- Berry development.
- Fruit quality.
- Normal yield potential.

Maintaining healthy foliage is especially important because strawberry fruit development depends on adequate functional leaf area.

## Management

### Before Planting
- Use certified or disease-free planting material from reliable nurseries.
- Select cultivars adapted to local growing conditions.
- Choose well-drained planting sites.
- Provide adequate sunlight.
- Maintain suitable spacing for airflow.
- Avoid planting visibly diseased material.
- Prepare the soil appropriately before planting.

### During Growth
- Monitor leaves, crowns, runners, flowers, and fruit regularly.
- Maintain appropriate soil moisture.
- Avoid prolonged drought stress.
- Avoid excessive irrigation and waterlogging.
- Maintain balanced plant nutrition.
- Control weeds that compete for water, nutrients, and light.
- Maintain adequate airflow around plants.
- Remove dead or diseased foliage where appropriate.
- Avoid unnecessary plant injury.
- Monitor plants closely during warm, wet weather.

### After Harvest
- Remove diseased or heavily damaged plant material where appropriate.
- Maintain field sanitation.
- Remove unwanted volunteer strawberry plants where necessary.
- Continue monitoring plants for late-season symptoms.
- Review cultivar and planting performance before the next production cycle.
- Manage old foliage according to the production system and local recommendations.

Specific biological-control products are not included in this record because a healthy crop does not require biological disease treatment.

## Chemical Management

No chemical disease treatment is required for a healthy strawberry crop in the absence of a diagnosed disease or pest problem.

Unnecessary pesticide applications should be avoided.

When a disease or pest problem is identified, treatment decisions should be based on:
- Confirmed diagnosis.
- Disease or pest risk.
- Crop growth stage.
- Cultivar susceptibility.
- Current local agricultural recommendations.
- Current pesticide labels and regulations.

**Important:** Chemical recommendations, permitted products, formulations, application rates, resistance-management requirements, pre-harvest intervals, and regulations vary by country and can change over time. KrishiVani should use current, locally applicable agricultural guidance before providing specific pesticide instructions.

## Resistant / Tolerant Varieties

Disease resistance is not relevant to the healthy classification itself.

When selecting strawberry cultivars, however, resistance or reduced susceptibility to locally important diseases can help maintain healthy plants and reduce disease-management requirements.

Specific resistance claims should be verified for the individual cultivar and local disease environment.

## Immediate Actions

When a strawberry plant appears healthy:

1. Continue routine monitoring.
2. Maintain appropriate soil moisture.
3. Maintain good soil drainage.
4. Maintain adequate sunlight and airflow.
5. Maintain balanced nutrition.
6. Control competing weeds.
7. Remove dead or diseased foliage where necessary.
8. Monitor leaves for spots, discoloration, or distortion.
9. Monitor fruit for rot, mold, lesions, or abnormal development.
10. Investigate persistent or newly developing abnormalities promptly.
